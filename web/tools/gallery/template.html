      HOW A RANDOM PILE OF NUMBERS LEARNS VOLLEYBALL

      No reinforcement learning, no gradients, no human games to copy. Just evolution: keep what wins, copy it with small mistakes, repeat a few hundred million times. Everything below is computed live from the real brains and the real runs.

      THE SHORT VERSION

      THE BRAIN

      A tiny neural network. 12 numbers in: where it is, where the ball is, where the opponent is, and how fast each moves, mirrored so one brain plays either side. 64 hidden units (softsign). 3 outputs: forward, back, jump, each pressed when its number is above zero.

      That is 1,027 weights. The weights are the whole player: no rules, no code about volleyball.

      THE MAP

      Evolution with a map: MAP-Elites. A 20 × 20 grid of play styles: how far back on its side a bot plays, and how much of the time it is in the air. Each of the 400 cells keeps the best brain found for that style.

      Children are mutated copies (a little noise on every weight) or mixes of two parents, and a child takes a cell when it scores better than the owner. The map keeps the weird styles alive, which is where the specialists came from.

      WHO YOU PLAY

      What a bot learns depends on who it trains against. Night one: a hand-written coach. Then a ladder: past champions, Elo-rated with the coach fixed at 1000.

      In the league, fitness is a soft minimum over the eight hardest opponents, so a bot cannot get rich farming the weak ones, and it must still beat the coach. A new champion has to win 55% head to head against the reigning one.

      THE ENGINE

      One physics kernel in Rust, compiled to WebAssembly, after David Ha's slimevolleygym. The trainer (Python, 12 workers, 6,000 to 15,000 matches a second) and this page run the same bytes.

      A match is just a serve (a 64-bit seed) and two brains. Every tick feeds a fingerprint, so a replay, here or there, either lands on the same hash or does not count.

      THE SPECIALISTS

      Twenty-minute runs with a rule they cannot break: never jump, always jump, only lobs, only spikes, juggle first, barely move, react like a human (one decision every 0.3 s).

      Each has a twin: the same start and seed with no rule, so the cost of the style is measured, not guessed. Soft and low (the Dinker) loses to everything. Barely moving (Lazy) costs nothing.

      THE BIG LESSON

      A twelve-hour league, with exploiters hunting its blind spots, filled the map with 400 strong bots and promoted none. Its best: official Elo 1819.

      Twenty minutes of nudges from the right starting bot, against the top four, made 2183: the boss you can play above. The only thing higher is the oracle, a hand-written bot that plans with the game's own physics (2220).
